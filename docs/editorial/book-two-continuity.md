# Book Two continuity constraints

Book One is finished and locked canon. Its manuscript, robot construction and laboratory history are unchanged. The following basement arrangement begins after its robot attack and is established in Book Two.

## Seth’s family and belonging — Chapter 1

Author-approved backstory: Seth’s mother hated living in Cleveland. When Seth was fourteen, she left to live with her sister in California. Since leaving, she sends a birthday card every year and calls him on Christmas. Neither her name nor his parents’ marital status, custody arrangements or additional family history is established. Her dislike of living in Cleveland does not establish that she hated Seth.

His father is quiet, hardworking and employed at a steel mill. Long hours keep him away from home much of the time. His absence comes from work; no cruelty, indifference, alcoholism or abandonment is established. Chapter 1’s quiet kitchen scene shows Seth doing homework while his father is still at work, with a kept birthday card nearby rather than a birthday celebration taking place.

Seth feels lonely both at home and at school. His new friends, the Defenders, give him companionship and belonging. Billy’s repeated tutoring appointments matter because someone expects Seth and makes time for him. These connections support the schoolwork story; Seth still earns graduation through sustained practice, study and his own examination work. The existing tutoring/graduation illustration and exact ending remain intact. His father’s graduation attendance is unspecified, with no reunion or deliberate refusal to attend added. This background does not explain or alter Seth’s strength or concentration rules; Book One and other chapters remain unchanged by this revision.

## Hidden basement workshop and routes

Author-confirmed: police searched Daniel’s original upstairs laboratory after the robot attack and found him gone. Daniel secretly returned beneath the same building. Luke’s iron jaws chewed through a disused drain and intervening masonry to make a concealed route from the sewer tunnels. Its opening is above the sewer water; the workshop stays dry. Rent preserves Daniel’s keys/access and lets him keep the upstairs shuttered and apparently abandoned. Book One’s robot was built upstairs, never retroactively in this basement.

The ordinary access route is street → alley → steps → sunken service yard → service door → front room (bench, sink clock, telephone) → workshop door behind movable storage shelving. Eleanor uses it in Chapter 9. Chapter 15’s invitation explicitly directs Jenny downstairs at the address she recognizes. Daniel moves the shelf and opens the workshop door in Chapter 16; leaving that sightline open in Chapter 17 lets Seth see Jenny from the service-door threshold after descending the steps. Her chair faces the inner doorway, with a partial view past the bench. Neither Seth nor Fist is given a view through masonry or a basement window from street level.

The rear workshop contains two restraint stations, its bench, the unfinished empty collector and a separate glass-protected control booth. A small window high in the basement wall above the bench faces the sunken yard. Luke flies out into the yard and then up to the roof to intercept Fist. Fist scouts from the rooftops, looking down toward the yard; the sign and railing keep their aerial fight out of Seth’s sight. The sewer passage is Daniel’s hidden return route, not the visitors’ entrance or an escape used through Chapter 18.

Daniel closes/latches the basement window after Luke leaves, then leaves the service door open as bait. The service door closes when Seth enters and gas affects both human captives. Luke waits with injured Fist outside the closed window. Daniel shuts off the gas, ventilates the room and only then leaves the booth and opens the window to admit them. Billy’s rendezvous is at the service-yard steps; he has not reached them when Chapter 18 ends.

## Billy’s delay and the Blackwells’ knowledge

Billy reports Jenny missing, gives police the basement directions and leaves his office to meet Seth. His delay is not explained by the police call alone. Eleanor and Claude’s limousine stops beside him; Claude’s offer of a lift is sincere. Billy reluctantly accepts because it appears quicker than walking. Eleanor asks the driver for a supposedly quick stop for papers at her campaign office, with more papers and questions while the driver waits, delays him. He asks twice to go directly to his appointment; Claude apologizes and offers to take him nearer. Billy finally gets out two streets short and hurries on foot. No fixed clock times or lengthy social visit are introduced.

Eleanor knows Billy is investigating Daniel, but she does not know Jenny has been kidnapped or that Seth and Fist have been captured. Claude is not knowingly complicit. Billy says only that he has an appointment, rather than telling them about the missing reporter or the rescue. He avoids making unsupported accusations in front of Claude: rent invoices establish payments, not knowing robot funding or responsibility for the earlier newspaper call/police interference. Those deeper links remain unproven to the heroes.

Author-confirmed: Daniel / Doc Dork and Luke / Jaws do **not** know Jenny Jenkins is Pink Blur as of Chapter 15. They know Jenny as the reporter whose byline appears in the newspaper and as Billy’s associate. Eleanor has told Daniel that Billy works with the Defenders.

Chapter 15’s trap relies on Billy calling the Defenders to help a missing reporter. It does not rely on Daniel or Luke knowing her powers, identity, or relationship with Seth. The newspaper photograph of a masked pink figure beside Zerdo in Chapter 14 does not identify that figure as Jenny.

For Chapter 16, Daniel cannot prepare a trap specifically to counter Jenny’s speed on the basis of current knowledge. Jenny may recognize Luke without him recognizing her superhero identity. Any discovery of her powers or identity must happen explicitly on the page; do not assume it has already occurred. The timing of that discovery remains for the author to decide.

Chapter 16 preserves the secret: Jenny recognizes Luke, but Daniel and Luke still regard her as an ordinary reporter. Gas incapacitates her before she achieves visible super speed. Daniel attributes her attempted escape to a stumble. Her civilian clothes remain on, and only Jenny is captive; the adjacent station and energy collector remain empty. Daniel waits for Billy’s response.

Jenny recognizes Dr. Orkleson from witnessing him beside the rampaging robot, barking orders, not from a newspaper photograph. In Chapter 16 she confronts him about it. He claims the robot developed a mind of its own, casts free energy as repayment for the damage, and thanks the Defenders through gritted teeth. These are Daniel’s self-serving claims, not verified narration. This exchange does not reveal Jenny’s superhero identity.

## Chapter 20: One Good Punch

Continues the published Chapter 19 (`The Empty Steps`) on main, not the archived deferred source. Luke initially watches Seth's partly bowed but closed right cuff; Billy's questioning draws Luke toward the inner doorway. Rolled-aside shelving screens Jenny's feet and cage target from Luke there; Daniel faces Billy at the open service threshold. Neither villain sees Jenny's plasma or learns she is Pink Blur.

Both ankle cuffs melt open; both wrist cuffs stay attached. A deliberate kick melts an upper cage opening away from the one injured glove. Fist squeezes free, surprises distracted Luke with one punch, leaves through the already-open high window (opened in Chapter 17 and never closed afterward), crosses the yard and rises to the roofs. Luke follows. Flaming Head rises from his exterior window-jamb position into Luke's path. Fist follows visible movements; neither he nor Luke understands energy messages. Flaming Head never enters.

With Luke outside, Seth finishes bending his right cuff, bends off his left, tears one ankle mounting free and bends the other cuff open. He then bends both Jenny wrist cuffs open. They walk hand in hand, with Billy, supported Fist and Flaming Head nearby, toward the shop to wait for help. The kiss does not delay care or erase Fist's injuries.
