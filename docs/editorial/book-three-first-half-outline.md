# Book Three — working outline

## Author rule — Flaming Head’s silent communication

Throughout Book Three, Flaming Head never produces an audible voice. His words arrive privately inside the minds of established recipients: Seth and Jenny, or Laura while their shared-energy connection remains. Daniel, Luke, Billy, Fist and bystanders cannot hear or overhear him. Seth and Jenny relay his messages aloud when Billy or Fist need them. Dialogue tags such as “said” describe mental communication, not sound; no illustration should add an audible speech effect. Laura retains her shared-energy connection; reduced power does not make his words public or give anybody new telepathy.


Book Three: Lava Lips now has a complete fourteen-chapter manuscript: Laura abandons Flaming Head and remains a powered villain, weakened Flaming Head returns with a warning, and the book ends on Dork’s satisfaction with the newly eager Jaws. Laura hides her reduced power; Jaws now admires her. The solar sacrifice, stolen vault, tank and Terminal Tower showdown belong to Book Four: Clash in Cleveland. Both titles are author-confirmed; Book Four’s later chapter divisions remain a working plan. Chapters 1–14 govern their scene details. This map supersedes the earlier Bob-based outlines and twenty-chapter single-book plan.

## 1. Someone, Please

Open with Laura Baxter crying beside the lake on the night of the original blast. Her boyfriend has dumped her. Waves crash against the shore, and water carrying the scattered energy enters her mouth. Stay with her experience rather than retelling the entire Book One event.

Later, brushing her teeth, she spits into the sink and burns a hole through it. She seeks help, but everything appears normal while her saliva remains inside her mouth. Once it leaves, it burns. Establish the frightening discovery and unsuccessful search for help through selected scenes, moving forward from the blast toward the present-day story.

Her inability to kiss anyone without hurting them deepens her isolation. Give her a life and personality beyond the breakup, with those details to be developed rather than assumed.

End with Laura asking the world for help, not knowing whether anyone can hear her.

### Chapter 1 art ideas

- **Lakefront heartbreak:** Laura in gray sweatpants, black T-shirt and glasses, messy brown hair blown across her face as she cries by the lake. A crashing wave and distant blast light place her within the original event without changing its established staging.
- **The sink:** a small bathroom, toothbrush and toothpaste nearby; Laura recoils as her spit burns through the porcelain. The glow comes from the sink, not from permanently flaming lips.
- **The plea:** Laura alone beside the lake later, looking outward as she asks for help. Emphasize loneliness and the open water; do not show Flaming Head beside her before he has sensed her need.

## 2. Needed Down the Lake

Return to the Defenders after Book Two. Seth and Jenny are practicing and improving; Fist is still healing. Perch remains away.

Flaming Head feels Laura's sadness and need. This is the author's specific new development of their shared-energy connection, not an established ability to read anyone's thoughts or locate anyone without limits.

He tells Seth and Jenny he is needed farther along the lake. They interpret for Billy and Fist. This is the departure already proposed in the earlier outline, now given Laura's plea as its cause rather than adding a second departure.

Seth asks, “There are more like us?” Let the question register his surprise and curiosity before Flaming Head leaves. Flaming Head can acknowledge the person whose distress he feels without claiming to know how many others were changed. Any reply reaches Seth and Jenny through the energy link; Billy and Fist need interpretation.

He leaves to find and help her. The Defenders know someone needs him, but do not yet know Laura's full story.

- **Departure:** Flaming Head heads out along the lakeshore, the others watching him go. A spacious composition links this image emotionally to Laura's plea without depicting a literal visible signal between them.

## 3. No Answer

Dork and Luke are living on the streets and keeping out of sight. Eleanor's funding has dried up, and she will not answer his calls.

Luke has lost faith in the promises about restoring his body. He challenges Daniel more openly. Daniel needs a plan that will get them out of their present circumstances and give Luke a reason to stay.

A newspaper announces that the Army will demonstrate a shrink/enlarge ray in Cleveland next week. Daniel imagines stolen machinery carried in his pocket, then restored wherever he wants it. A tank could supply weapons and parts.

End with his decision to steal it, and Luke asking how this helps him.

### Chapter 3 art ideas

- **No answer:** Dork at a public pay phone, shabby belongings close by, Luke hovering nearby with an impatient expression. An ordinary Cleveland street makes their reduced circumstances clear.
- **The discovery:** Dork crouches over the newspaper announcement while Luke watches skeptically. Any readable headline must match the eventual manuscript; no invented demonstration date, Army unit or technical specifications.

Use period equipment: CRT computers, wired cameras, video monitors, VHS recorders, telephones and a police-band receiver. Their reach depends on actual cameras, connections and records.

Eleanor bites her tongue. She hates Claude's generosity toward the Defenders, but stays quiet. She is keeping a low profile and seems occupied with something of her own; her scheme remains unrevealed.

In a separate scene, Flaming Head reaches Laura. His ability to understand what has happened offers her an initial reason to hope.

## 5. Lava Lips

Flaming Head tries to teach Laura to control and use her ability to help people. Establish that she can spit burning plasma, with its practical limits still to be developed.

After Laura fears backlash over her poor aim and being identified, Flaming Head gives her a costume through the same golden-flame transformation he used for Seth and Jenny. The disguise does not fix her aim or her saliva. Costume: a fitted black bodysuit with flames up the sides, a ponytail and a black eye mask. Her everyday clothes are gray sweatpants, a black T-shirt and glasses, with messy brown hair.

Training gives her something to attempt, but it does not remove her despair or solve the intimacy she has lost. Show the difference between learning to aim a dangerous power and finding a way to live with it.

Possible emotional turn: Laura begins blaming Flaming Head for cursing her with the affliction. He is teaching her to use something she wants gone. Her hurt hardens into anger at him, and she wants revenge. This is her understanding of what happened, not proof that he deliberately harmed her. Whether she explicitly asks him for a cure and what he can honestly tell her remain to be developed.

Author-approved naming: Laura accidentally sneezes burning droplets onto a newsstand. She helps the seller get clear and stays through the emergency response, but the stand is destroyed. A newspaper headline calls her “Lava Lips.” The account includes the accident and her help; she experiences the headline as humiliating. Dork learns of her through that coverage. Luke can later use the already-public name sarcastically in a jealous outburst.

Dork hears about her and sees someone who could be useful. End with his interest in finding her.

## 6. A Tank in His Pocket

Expanded chapter plan: intercut Dork rebuilding his material power with Laura discovering that other people's fear gives her power. Over several days, her anger at Flaming Head hardens and her guilt about the newsstand fades. Dork does not approach or influence her in this chapter. His approach remains Chapter 7.

### Scene 1 — The photograph facing out

Open with Laura at home after the argument. The newspaper no longer lies folded to hide her photograph. She studies the flames behind her masked face. At first she remembers the seller's distress; then she returns to the headline and the humiliation of being named by strangers.

She remembers Flaming Head admitting that his landing changed her. He has offered practice and a costume, but cannot restore the ordinary life she wanted. In her anger she begins treating those gifts as attempts to make her accept what he did. This is her interpretation; he did not deliberately curse her.

She puts on the costume to go out. Previously she wore it because she feared being recognized and pursued. Now she wants to see what happens when people recognize Lava Lips. Keep the existing Chapter 5 mirror illustration unchanged; a new mirror scene need not be illustrated.

### Scene 2 — The Army demonstration

Cut to Dork and Luke at the advertised demonstration. A cordoned outdoor display area contains the ray, a test object and an unoccupied tank. A soldier reduces the test object and restores it on a marked clear patch. Make the control change between shrinking and enlarging visible to Daniel and the reader.

Establish the theft opportunity through the actual layout: Daniel joins the spectators while Luke stays out of sight behind the display. The ray is portable, with controls Daniel can operate after watching the demonstration. Its exact appearance is a proposed design until drafted and illustrated consistently.

Luke asks whether this gets them closer to recovering his body. Daniel says they need equipment first. Luke has heard that before, but still wants the plan to work.

### Scene 3 — People make room

Laura walks a busy shopping street in costume, coat open enough for the flame pattern to show. Someone recognizes her from the photograph and says, “Lava Lips.” A conversation stops. A pedestrian steps off her path; a shopkeeper holds the door but keeps his distance.

Her first reaction is the old sting of being stared at. Then she notices that nobody laughs, crowds her or tells her to move. She slows down instead of hurrying away. At a small shop counter, the customer ahead abandons their place and the clerk serves her with nervous haste. She pays normally, but lets the silence stretch because she likes having everyone's attention.

No plasma is needed. Her reputation does the work. People fear an accident as well as an attack; their retreat does not establish that she can control every discharge.

### Scene 4 — The tank disappears

Return to the demonstration after the public test. As the equipment is being prepared for removal, Luke draws the soldiers' attention away from the ray by appearing at the far end of the display. Their reaction gives Daniel a brief opening rather than making the entire security detail vanish.

Daniel takes the ray, shrinks the empty tank and pockets it. The soldiers discover him before he can leave unnoticed. Luke breaks away from the distraction and rejoins him during their escape through the display's service exit. Fix sight lines, distances and pursuit in the draft so the ray does not become a solution to every obstacle. No shrinking people and no passenger inside the tank.

Luke expects thanks. Daniel is already talking about what the tank will let him build.

### Scene 5 — The burned stand

On a later walk, Laura returns to the remains of the newsstand. The seller is sorting damaged fittings beside the blackened counter. He recognizes the costume and stops working. She remembers pulling him clear, but this time the memory brings no urge to apologize.

She thinks about how quickly everyone found a name for her and how little the headline cared that she helped. The seller's ruined livelihood becomes something she pushes out of her thoughts: nobody was hurt; it was an accident; Flaming Head's landing made it possible.

She notices that the seller is watching her mouth. Instead of reassuring him, she lets him watch. She walks away without offering help. The significant change is that she no longer feels bad about the fire and enjoys being the person he is afraid of. Do not rewrite the original fire as deliberate or turn the seller into someone who deserved it.

### Scene 6 — A laboratory he can carry

Dork returns covertly to the original upstairs laboratory. Proposed transport solution: he shrinks surviving freestanding equipment, benches and storage cabinets individually, packs them into a case and leaves the building shell and attached utilities behind. He does not detach or carry the upstairs room itself.

He takes them to a vacant ground-floor industrial workshop with a wide loading entrance and room for the tank. Establish how he gains access; the vacancy does not give him legitimate ownership. Restore each item on a clear patch, then enlarge the tank with its hatch empty. The tank remains a source of weapons and parts; it does not instantly become an unexplained power supply for the lab.

Police still hold the confiscated Book Two basement apparatus, papers, cage and restraints. Nothing in this move recovers Luke's original body or demonstrates a way to restore him.

Flaming Head finds Laura near the lakeside place where they previously agreed to meet; he does not track her thoughts across the city. She arrives in costume, carrying herself differently. He asks whether she wants to talk. She tells him people recognize her now and get out of her way.

He keeps his distance. She deliberately steps toward him and watches him move back. She does not attack him or acquire a new ability; she discovers she likes making even him give ground. ### Scene 8 — Two kinds of power

Close in the relocated workshop. Daniel has the tank, machinery and a working place again. Luke asks when they start on his body. Daniel postpones the answer and lays out the Lava Lips article on a restored bench.

## 7. Dork's Offer

Full chapter outline following the author's new direction: Daniel approaches Laura alone. Luke stays in the relocated workshop and does not meet her until Daniel brings her back. Luke distrusts anyone with powers from the lake energy and initially suspects Laura is working with Flaming Head and the Defenders. His suspicion is his belief, not proof of an actual conspiracy. Their antagonism begins before his jealous outburst.

### Scene 1 — Someone who does not move aside

A few days after Chapter 6, Daniel finds costumed Laura on the shopping street described in the newsstand report. Asking local shopkeepers about her conspicuous public appearances gives him a concrete way to locate her. Luke remains at the workshop; Daniel has told him only that he is going to find the woman from the paper.

Daniel approaches while other people give her space. He addresses her without the usual frightened hesitation. Laura initially enjoys making him wait, then becomes curious when he holds his ground. He knows her public nickname and the reported fire, not her civilian name or private history.

Daniel asks about the power. Laura bristles at being treated as another demonstration. His first attempt to interest her in useful applications fails; Flaming Head already tried that approach.

### Scene 2 — What he can offer

Laura asks why she should join him. Daniel can mention Flaming Head as his own known enemy, but must let Laura reveal her grievance rather than displaying unexplained knowledge of it.

Her desire is to make Flaming Head helpless. Daniel does not promise that draining him will cure her, restore intimacy or remove the other characters' powers. If she asks whether it changes her, he cannot establish that it will. What wins her attention is revenge.

### Scene 4 — One of them

Luke argues that her power comes from the same source as the people who have opposed them. Laura's admission that Flaming Head trained her and gave her the suit makes him more suspicious. He thinks the arrival could be a way for their enemies to find the new lab.

>

Daniel dismisses him instead of listening. Laura resents being questioned and deliberately stays where she is. Laura's insult sharpens Luke's existing suspicion into personal resentment. He has helped rebuild the lab, yet his uncle welcomes someone who calls him a monstrosity. His concern is not only jealousy, and his suspicion does not make him correct.

### Scene 5 — The promise on the bench

Daniel turns the encounter into a tour of a newly assembled collector prototype. Over the days since the move, he has begun rebuilding from his earlier research using surviving upstairs components. The original collector, cage, restraints and papers remain in police custody. This is new apparatus, not recovered confiscated machinery.

It sits on a wheeled trolley above a small control box with two round analog meters. One thick cable is initially disconnected; Daniel fits the connector after Laura leaves. This is separate from the gray handheld Army shrink/enlarge ray with its copper-ringed barrel and red/blue controls. No successful drain or confirmed range is established.

Daniel claims it can drain Flaming Head. He has no demonstrated success against him and avoids a live test. A powered meter or glowing indicator must not be presented as proof that the claim works. Laura is impressed by the machinery and the certainty of his explanation.

Luke recognizes the salesmanship. Daniel once sounded just as certain about restoring his body. He asks about that work; Daniel tells him this opportunity comes first. In front of Laura, the dismissal stings more.

### Scene 6 — Her decision

Laura asks what Daniel actually needs her to do. Proposed answer: help bring Flaming Head within reach of the collector when Daniel has prepared it. He believes their previous connection makes her useful. The trap's location and mechanics remain for later plotting; no limitless range or automatic immobilization is established here.

She accepts because she wants to see Flaming Head lose his power and confidence. She does not agree out of gratitude to Daniel or romantic interest. She also wants to see whether the device delivers, giving her an ongoing reason to remain involved.

Luke calls her “Lava Lips” with deliberate sarcasm. She answers rather than retreating: “You sound frightened.” This turns the pleasure she took in strangers' fear toward him. Daniel stops the argument by defending her usefulness and sending Luke back to another task.

No kiss, immunity test, plasma attack or electrical accident occurs in this chapter. Luke remains Luke, with his memories and grievance intact until the late rooftop accident.
