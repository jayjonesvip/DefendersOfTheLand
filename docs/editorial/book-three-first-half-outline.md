# Book Three — first-half outline

Current working outline following the author's decision to center Laura Baxter / Lava Lips and remove Bob. Supersedes the earlier Bob-based first-half and opening outlines. Titles and later chapter divisions are provisional. Chapters 1–5 are now drafted; their current manuscripts govern scene details.

## 1. Someone, Please

Open with Laura Baxter crying beside the lake on the night of the original blast. Her boyfriend has dumped her. Waves crash against the shore, and water carrying the scattered energy enters her mouth. Stay with her experience rather than retelling the entire Book One event.

Later, brushing her teeth, she spits into the sink and burns a hole through it. She seeks help, but everything appears normal while her saliva remains inside her mouth. Once it leaves, it burns. Establish the frightening discovery and unsuccessful search for help through selected scenes, moving forward from the blast toward the present-day story.

Her inability to kiss anyone without hurting them deepens her isolation. Give her a life and personality beyond the breakup, with those details to be developed rather than assumed.

End with Laura asking the world for help, not knowing whether anyone can hear her.

### Chapter 1 art ideas

- **Lakefront heartbreak:** Laura in gray sweatpants, black T-shirt and glasses, messy brown hair blown across her face as she cries by the lake. A crashing wave and distant blast light place her within the original event without changing its established staging.
- **The sink:** a small bathroom, toothbrush and toothpaste nearby; Laura recoils as her spit burns through the porcelain. The glow comes from the sink, not from permanently flaming lips.
- **The plea:** Laura alone beside the lake later, looking outward as she asks for help. Emphasize loneliness and the open water; do not show Flaming Head beside her before he has sensed her need.

## 2. Needed Down the Lake

Return to the Defenders after Book Two. Seth and Jenny are practicing and improving; Fist is still healing. Perch remains away.

Flaming Head feels Laura's sadness and need. This is the author's specific new development of their shared-energy connection, not an established ability to read anyone's thoughts or locate anyone without limits.

He tells Seth and Jenny he is needed farther along the lake. They interpret for Billy and Fist. This is the departure already proposed in the earlier outline, now given Laura's plea as its cause rather than adding a second departure.

Seth asks, “There are more like us?” Let the question register his surprise and curiosity before Flaming Head leaves. Flaming Head can acknowledge the person whose distress he feels without claiming to know how many others were changed. Any reply reaches Seth and Jenny through the energy link; Billy and Fist need interpretation.

He leaves to find and help her. The Defenders know someone needs him, but do not yet know Laura's full story.

### Chapter 2 art ideas

- **“There are more like us?”** Seth turns toward Flaming Head with surprise, Jenny listening beside him. Billy and recovering Fist are present but cannot understand the energy message directly. Use their established appearances and the pre-facility setting.
- **Departure:** Flaming Head heads out along the lakeshore, the others watching him go. A spacious composition links this image emotionally to Laura's plea without depicting a literal visible signal between them.

## 3. No Answer

Dork and Luke are living on the streets and keeping out of sight. Eleanor's funding has dried up, and she will not answer his calls.

Luke has lost faith in the promises about restoring his body. He challenges Daniel more openly. Daniel needs a plan that will get them out of their present circumstances and give Luke a reason to stay.

A newspaper announces that the Army will demonstrate a shrink/enlarge ray in Cleveland next week. Daniel imagines stolen machinery carried in his pocket, then restored wherever he wants it. A tank could supply weapons and parts.

End with his decision to steal it, and Luke asking how this helps him.

### Chapter 3 art ideas

- **No answer:** Dork at a public pay phone, shabby belongings close by, Luke hovering nearby with an impatient expression. An ordinary Cleveland street makes their reduced circumstances clear.
- **The discovery:** Dork crouches over the newspaper announcement while Luke watches skeptically. Any readable headline must match the eventual manuscript; no invented demonstration date, Army unit or technical specifications.

## 4. A Place of Their Own

Claude has enjoyed the newspaper stories about the Defenders. He donates a facility with a training room, library, computers and surveillance equipment advanced for 1987.

Let the tour establish a home as well as a useful base. Claude has even bought Fist-O-Mite a locker to sleep in, with a comfortable cushion. Seth and Jenny have somewhere to practice; Billy has research and operations space.

Use period equipment: CRT computers, wired cameras, video monitors, VHS recorders, telephones and a police-band receiver. Their reach depends on actual cameras, connections and records.

Eleanor bites her tongue. She hates Claude's generosity toward the Defenders, but stays quiet. She is keeping a low profile and seems occupied with something of her own; her scheme remains unrevealed.

Chapter 4 author-directed detail: Eleanor studies Pink Blur as though she recognizes her from somewhere. Jenny notices and becomes conscious of her mask. Claude interrupts before Eleanor says anything. This creates suspicion without establishing that Eleanor knows Jenny's identity.

In a separate scene, Flaming Head reaches Laura. His ability to understand what has happened offers her an initial reason to hope.

### Chapter 4 art ideas

- **The gift:** exterior establishing view of Claude welcoming the Defenders to the red-brick facility. Use the existing facility concept as a design reference, with final architecture still subject to review. Keep Seth and Jenny's public presentation consistent with what Claude knows.
- **Testing the scanner:** Billy leans over the operations desk and adjusts the police-band receiver while a dispatch comes through. Period CRT monitors glow nearby, and the others pause to listen.
- **A place for Fist:** a closer interior scene of Claude showing Fist his cushioned sleeping locker. The glove's reaction supplies the emotional focus; preserve his single-glove anatomy and recovering cuff.
- **First contact:** a separate lakefront scene of Flaming Head finding civilian Laura. His warm light contrasts with her guarded expression. This is an encounter, not an instant cure.

## 5. Lava Lips

Flaming Head tries to teach Laura to control and use her ability to help people. Establish that she can spit burning plasma, with its practical limits still to be developed.

Introduce her costume during this period: a fitted black bodysuit with flames up the sides, a ponytail and a black eye mask. Her everyday clothes are gray sweatpants, a black T-shirt and glasses, with messy brown hair.

Training gives her something to attempt, but it does not remove her despair or solve the intimacy she has lost. Show the difference between learning to aim a dangerous power and finding a way to live with it.

Possible emotional turn: Laura begins blaming Flaming Head for cursing her with the affliction. He is teaching her to use something she wants gone. Her hurt hardens into anger at him, and she wants revenge. This is her understanding of what happened, not proof that he deliberately harmed her. Whether she explicitly asks him for a cure and what he can honestly tell her remain to be developed.

Author-approved naming: Laura accidentally sneezes burning droplets onto a newsstand. She helps the seller get clear and stays through the emergency response, but the stand is destroyed. A newspaper headline calls her “Lava Lips.” The account includes the accident and her help; she experiences the headline as humiliating. Dork learns of her through that coverage. Luke can later use the already-public name sarcastically in a jealous outburst.

Dork hears about her and sees someone who could be useful. End with his interest in finding her.

### Chapter 5 art ideas — integrated

- **Learning to aim:** civilian Laura spits a small plasma shot at a baking sheet backed by bare earth, Flaming Head safely to one side.
- **The costume:** Laura studies the black flame-sided bodysuit, ponytail and eye mask in her bedroom mirror.
- **The newsstand fire:** masked Laura pulls the seller clear while a bystander brings an extinguisher. The fire follows an accidental sneeze, not an intentional attack.
- **The anger:** civilian Laura holds her glasses and newspaper while confronting Flaming Head about what the affliction has taken from her.
- **Dork takes notice:** Dork studies the naming headline and masked photograph, with bodyless Luke watching and the Army announcement beside him.

## 6. A Tank in His Pocket

At the Army demonstration, show the ray shrinking equipment and restoring it. Establish the setting and opportunity that make Dork and Luke's theft possible.

They steal the ray and shrink a tank, which Daniel carries away. There is no sleeping passenger: Bob is removed from this version.

Dork returns covertly to his original upstairs laboratory, shrinks what remains, transports it and restores it elsewhere. The draft must establish exactly what structure or contents he moves and how they are separated from the surrounding building.

He enlarges the tank successfully. He has a base, machinery and parts again. Police still hold Book Two's confiscated basement equipment.

Luke helped make this possible, but his original body remains missing and Daniel still has no demonstrated way to restore him.

### Chapter 6 art ideas

- **The Army demonstration:** equipment reduced before a watching crowd, with period military vehicles and a clear demonstration area. Final ray design, security and geography must follow the draft.
- **A tank in his pocket:** Dork holds the miniature tank between his fingers while Luke hovers beside him. Keep the tank's model and relative size consistent with the later full-size vehicle. No Bob or other passenger.
- **The relocated lab:** a wide reveal of the restored laboratory and full-size tank at the new site. Illustrate only after the lab's transport boundaries and destination are settled; no reappearance of confiscated basement equipment.

## 7. Dork's Offer

Dork approaches Laura after hearing the stories. He finds her at the moment her anger at Flaming Head has made her receptive to someone who will take her grievance seriously. Show how he finds her; do not assume he overheard a private argument or knows its contents without learning them.

Laura joins forces with him. A possible appeal is the chance to act on her desire for revenge against Flaming Head. Dork sees her power as useful and can encourage her blame; the specific offer and any promises remain to be developed. Her anger gives the alliance a personal motive, while the decision to join remains hers.

Flaming Head learns of the alliance. Someone he tried to help is now working with a man who has already endangered his friends. This is the concrete cause of his alarm.

Luke resents Daniel's growing attention to Laura. Once again his uncle is excited about a new possibility while postponing the promise that kept Luke beside him.

During a jealous outburst, Luke can call her “Lava Lips” sarcastically, using the newspaper nickname he knows bothers her. The paper has already coined it in Chapter 5. Exact dialogue and her response remain to be drafted.

End with the new alliance formed and Luke's resentment close to breaking.

### Chapter 7 art ideas

- **The offer:** Dork speaks with Laura while Luke hangs back, watching their growing understanding. Laura's expression should show active consideration rather than unquestioning obedience. Setting and civilian/costume choice depend on how Dork finds her.
- **Left out:** a composition through Luke's viewpoint, with Dork's attention directed toward Laura and Luke visually separated. Do not illustrate a romantic connection between Dork and Laura; none is established.

## 8. Jaws O' Death

Luke confronts Daniel over Laura and the repeated broken promises. During the conflict he bites the shrink/enlarge ray.

An electrical discharge throws him across the laboratory. Show the immediate aftermath and establish the device's condition before it is used again.

He awakens without his memory of being Luke. He identifies only as Jaws O' Death, likes his iron teeth and floating appearance, and thinks he looks cool. This is not the first use of the name, which already exists in Book One.

Jaws develops a liking for Laura. Her response remains open. His new affection follows the accident; it does not erase the loss of the nephew who wanted his body back.

Dork now has his team: himself, Jaws and Lava Lips. No body transfer has occurred, and no safe kiss is established by Jaws's iron teeth.

### Chapter 8 art ideas

- **The bite:** Luke's iron jaws close on the device and an electrical arc throws him away. Keep the beam distinct from Laura's orange plasma and stage everyone clear of the discharge.
- **“I look cool”:** afterward, Jaws studies his reflection in a reflective lab surface, delighted by his appearance. Dork and Laura react behind him. This is a proposed visual staging for his changed attitude, not an added physical transformation.
- **The new team:** Dork, Jaws and Lava Lips together in the lab. Jaws's attention drifts toward Laura; her response stays ambiguous. No kiss, Bob or recovered body.

## 9. Cleveland's Defenders

At the donated facility, a police-radio call reports a bank robbery. Enough time and visible recovery have passed for Fist to contribute safely.

The Defenders respond as a well-oiled machine. Billy assesses and coordinates with police, Seth protects people and blocks escape, Jenny uses speed to clear civilians and interrupt threats, and Fist covers the remaining opening. Stage the bank clearly so each action supports the others.

Police take custody of the robbers. Witnesses see the team's precision and care. Jenny writes about the success using checked accounts while preserving her own identity and Seth's.

Follow the full bank scene with brief scenes of other calls, rescues and reports. The implication is sustained effective crime-fighting, rather than one isolated victory.

Cleveland is enjoying a renaissance: less crime, a clean lake, renewed waterfront life and a celebratory mood. Claude sees his generosity helping the city. The team enjoys the progress it has earned.

Intercut Dork studying the coverage and observing the Defenders through information he can actually obtain. He waits for the best time to weaken them. Their coordinated success makes him cautious.

### Chapter 9 art ideas

- **The call:** the team reacts to the police-band radio in the 1987 operations room. CRTs and wired video equipment establish the period; they do not display unexplained live footage from inside the bank.
- **Working together:** the bank robbery's main action composition shows Seth protecting civilians, Pink Blur moving through a clear route and Fist covering an escape, with Billy positioned to coordinate. Match the final action sequence rather than compressing incompatible moments into one image.
- **Cleveland on the rise:** a clean, active lakefront with people enjoying the waterfront and a newsstand carrying the team's latest success. Avoid fabricated crime statistics or unreadably elaborate article text.
- **The watcher:** Dork studies newspaper clippings in his relocated lab. Public reports, not unexplained headquarters surveillance feeds, supply his information.

## 10. The Return

At the height of this confidence, Flaming Head comes back visibly worried and upset.

Jenny and Seth understand his energy messages. Billy and Fist need them to relay his account. Perch remains away.

Flaming Head tells them about Laura: the woman he heard asking for help, the power she could not escape, his attempts to teach her, and her decision to join Dork.

The reader has seen the alliance forming; the tension here comes from the Defenders learning what it means. Flaming Head knows how powerful she is and fears what Daniel will encourage her to do.

End the first half with the team absorbing the warning. Their city is flourishing, but Dork has rebuilt his operation and gained someone their friend could not reach. Dork continues watching for his opening; whether this warning and the team's response supply it remains for the second half.

### Chapter 10 art ideas

- **The return:** Flaming Head hovers outside an open facility window, visibly distressed, while Seth and Jenny turn toward him. Keep flames clear of curtains, books and equipment; establish the arrival route in the draft.
- **The warning:** a closer group scene with Seth and Jenny listening directly, Billy and Fist waiting for their explanation. Reflect the change from celebration to concern without making the energy message visible as ordinary speech to everyone.
- **Closing image:** the team gathered in the facility after hearing the warning, the familiar room newly subdued. Laura and Dork need not appear as a literal vision or hologram; the reader already knows their alliance.

## Continuity and open development

- Laura's origin occurs on the original blast night. Chapter 1 moves forward through her discovery and search for help before her present-day plea; the main Book Three story follows Book Two. Do not place the entire opening sequence after Book Two.
- Flaming Head feeling Laura's sadness is author-directed new material. Keep its scope specific instead of assuming universal mind reading or unlimited tracking. Seth, Jenny and Perch understand his energy messages; Billy and Fist do not.
- Laura's real name is Laura Baxter. Her plasma comes from saliva leaving her mouth, not unrestricted fire breath. Medical help, daily coping, power limits, public incidents and Dork's persuasive offer need concrete development.
- Bob and his permanent shrinking are omitted from this version. The Army device, stolen tank and relocated lab remain.
- Establish what survives in the upstairs lab, how it can be transported and where it is restored. Book Two's collector, papers, cage and restraints remain in police custody.
- Establish the ray's controls and limits and its condition after the bite. No successful body transfer or recovery of Luke's original body is established.
- Jaws liking Laura is the current direction; her feelings and any relationship remain open. His teeth do not automatically protect him from her plasma.
- Daniel knows Seth's identity. He and Luke do not know Jenny is Pink Blur. Knowledge does not automatically pass to Laura, and Luke's memory loss does not erase Daniel's memories.
- Fist heals gradually. Seth and Jenny improve established abilities through practice. Perch remains absent. Seth's family history stays unchanged.
- Claude's gift is sincere and does not establish knowledge of civilian identities or involvement in Eleanor's dealings. Eleanor remains wealthy and influential; her private scheme is undecided.
- Dork's information comes from reporting and shown observation. The eventual attack and the opening he exploits remain undecided.
- The facility illustration is a visual concept, not an approved architectural plan. Chapter titles and the ten-chapter division remain flexible.

## Art direction across the first half

Illustration ideas are proposals, not generated or approved chapter assets. Keep the established warm, detailed inked comic style and consistent 1987 settings. Match current canonical character references: Jenny has green eyes, Seth blue eyes, Fist is one animated red boxing glove, and Jaws is a bodyless head with iron jaws. Track costumes, injuries, ray damage and miniature-object sizes scene by scene. Laura's burning plasma originates in saliva leaving her mouth; flame patterns on her costume are decoration. Preserve all earlier concept originals when making later versions.
