Off Comes the Top
Defenders of the Land, Book 4: Clash in Cleveland. Chapter 12, revision draft
Seth's hands closed around the crooked turret.
Inside the hull, Dork slapped the dial on his firing panel.
Click.
Red to blue.
The copper rings began to hum.
[IMAGE: Seth gripping the damaged turret rim, Dork's hand on the dial below, blue light spilling from the copper rings toward Terminal Tower.]
A motor whined under Seth's palms. The turret ring turned on its own, dragging the barrel back toward the skyline.
Seth dug in his boots.
The turret kept turning. His heels skidded a foot across the pavement.
"Jenny!"
"Going!"
She was already running for the hull.
"Say goodbye to your tower!" Dork shouted.
Seth wrenched sideways with everything he had.
The barrel jerked off its line. Dork fired.
Blue light tore across the square. It missed the tower by a full block, raked along the curb and clipped the top of a streetlamp.
The lamp head shrank to the size of a coffee cup. It dropped, rang off the pavement and rolled into the gutter.
"Heads down!" Billy yelled.
The beam swept over the stone planter and winked out.
Seth hauled the barrel skyward. His shoulders burned.
Flaming Head could have melted that ring in a second.
Flaming Head wasn't here.
Click.
Seth went still.
The dial. After every shot it snapped back to red, and Dork had to flip it to blue again. Seth counted the gap.
Two seconds.
Not much.
Enough.
[IMAGE: Close-up of the firing panel. The dial snapping from blue back to red, Dork's thumb poised over it, the streetlamp's tiny head rolling in the gutter behind.]
Above the rooftops, Fist banked around a chimney. Jaws stayed on his tail.
"You really don't know me," Fist said.
Jaws snapped at the air beside him. "Should I?"
The teeth clanged shut an inch from the cuff.
Fist slid away.
"No."
It came out quieter than he meant.
Jaws didn't notice. His eyes had slid past him, down to the street.
To the police car. To the woman beside the open door.
"Hey!" Jaws called. "Lady in the car! You want out?"
Laura looked up.
She smiled.
"About time."
Below, Dork slammed the reverse lever.
The tracks roared. The tank lurched backward, and Jenny, halfway up the hull, went off it like a cork.
She hit the pavement, rolled and came up with both white gloves scraped gray.
"I'm fine!" she called, before anyone could ask.
The tank kept pulling away. Seth kept his grip on the turret.
The tracks tore at the street. Chunks of asphalt flew. The engine screamed.
Seth stayed where he was.
"Where do you think you're going?"
Bolts cracked around the turret ring.
Steel shrieked.
The whole top of the tank came up in his hands.
[IMAGE: Seth heaving the full turret overhead as the tank strains backward, asphalt spraying, bolts flying, Dork's startled face in the open hull.]
A black cable stretched beneath it. It pulled out of its socket and dangled loose.
Dork jabbed the firing control. The copper opening stayed dark.
He looked up through the empty ring.
"Your gun's come loose," Seth said.
Dork's hand shot to a spare lead clipped inside the hull. He jammed it against the dangling cable.
Sparks spat. The copper rings flickered a weak blue.
"Jenny!"
"I see it!"
Seth swung the turret skyward. A thin blue thread leapt out of it, sailed over the rooftops and vanished.
Somewhere high above, a pigeon dropped four feet and kept flying.
The lead burned through. The rings went dark.
Jenny reached the driver's opening.
Her white glove closed around Dork's wrist.
"Out."
He snatched his other hand toward the controls. She caught that one too.
"Do you know what that cost?" Dork snarled.
"You stole it."
She hauled him through the hatch and onto the street. His shoes scraped the armor all the way down.
Billy waved two officers around the planter.
"This way. Keep him behind cover."
An officer fastened cuffs behind Dork's back. The other took his arm.
Seth lowered the turret to the pavement, copper opening pointed at the sky.
Jenny knelt beside it. The ray's metal box rattled in its cradle. She pried the clips loose, slid it out in one pull and carried it to Billy.
"Don't touch the controls."
Billy carried it away, an officer at his elbow.
Above the square, Jaws dived.
He dropped like a stone toward the police car, teeth wide, aimed at the door hinges.
"Laura! Duck!"
Laura didn't duck.
She leaned out past the officer's arm and spat.
A glob of lava hit the stack of wooden boards beside the planter. They burst into flame.
The officer with the extinguisher dived toward the fire.
His partner pulled Laura back from the door. She was still smiling.
Fist cut across the dive.
His knuckles struck the side of the iron jaw.
Jaws flew sideways, away from the car, and slammed into the cleared square. He rolled, whirled and came back up.
Fist hung in the air between him and the police.
"You'll have to get past me."
Jaws stared at him.
"Gladly."
[IMAGE: Laura leaning from the cruiser door, a lava glob arcing toward the boards; Fist crossing the frame mid-punch; Jaws frozen in his dive, teeth open.]
Seth stood over the dead turret.
The tank sat below him, headless, its engine ticking.
"That's all of it?" Jenny said.
Seth looked at the open ring. No copper. No machine. Only steel.
"That's all of it."
She checked the street.
Dork and his guards were clear. Billy and the ray were clear. The officers had Laura behind her door.
She stepped back.
"All yours."
Seth took the empty turret shell and lifted it overhead.
Behind the planter, Dork stopped struggling.
"No! That's an Army tank!"
Seth looked at the hull.
"I know."
He brought the shell down.
The crash rolled between the buildings. The hull folded beneath the blow. One track snapped loose and spilled across the pavement. The other buckled under the weight.
Dust burst around Seth's boots.
He let the turret settle into the crushed armor and stepped back.
The engine coughed once.
Then it stopped.
[IMAGE: Low-angle shot of the turret shell crashing onto the hull, armor folding, one track whipping free, dust and sparks, Terminal Tower rising behind.]
The dust hung over the wreck like fog.
Jaws turned at the crash.
He saw Dork behind the planter. He saw the folded steel. He saw the empty ring where the turret had been.
His eyes went back to the glove.
"That's it," he said. "I'm done playing."
He came in fast, teeth open.
Fist waited.
Closer.
The white cuff twitched.
Jaws stopped dead.
He had been about to bite. Instead he hung there, a foot from the glove, eyes narrowed.
"No," he said. "I don't think I will."
Fist didn't move.
He's learning.
He let the cuff drift lower. Close to the crushed hull. Close to a torn edge of steel.
Jaws's mean streak did the rest.
He lunged.
Fist dropped away.
The iron teeth slammed into the folded armor and screeched along it. Jaws jerked back with a howl, his jaw buzzing.
Fist came up beneath him and drove upward with everything in the glove.
His knuckles caught the iron jaw.
Jaws flew out of the dust and hit clear pavement. He skidded to a stop beside the wreck.
For a moment his eyes would not focus.
Fist hovered above him, cuff safely out of reach.
[IMAGE: Fist driving upward in a gap between dust clouds, the white cuff bright, Jaws's teeth scraping a torn steel edge on the wrecked hull.]
Jenny arrived with the towing chain.
"Keep him there."
Before Jaws could open his teeth, she passed the chain around the closed metal jaw. Seth came beside her and pulled it snug against the iron plates, leaving Jaws's nose and eyes clear.
They fastened the short tether to the tank's undamaged rear towing eye.
Jaws tried to rise. The chain drew tight.
He tried his teeth. The links held the halves together.
Seth kept a hand on the chain. Fist did not move away.
An officer called for equipment to move him under guard.
The last boards burned out behind the planter. The officer with the extinguisher put his foot on the final ember.
Across the street, Laura watched Jenny walk back to her escort.
"Still want me on your team?"
Jenny looked at the scorched boards. Then at her.
"If you want to help."
Laura looked away. She gave no answer.
Nobody hurried her into an unguarded car.
Billy stepped out from behind the planter, the ray safe beside an officer. He crouched, picked the toy-sized lamp head out of the gutter and slipped it into his coat pocket.
"The Army can put that back on its pole," he said.
Dork sat between his guards and glared at the tank.
Fist watched Jaws. Seth held the tether.
[IMAGE: Wide aftermath. The wrecked tank, chained Jaws, Dork in cuffs, Billy holding the toy lamp head, Laura at the cruiser, the burned boards, Terminal Tower glowing in afternoon light.]
Above them, every window of Terminal Tower caught the sun.
Seth looked up.
Still there.
END OF CHAPTER 12

• Chapter 13 continuity: the ray ends disconnected and safe with Billy; Jaws is chained by the jaw and tethered to the wreck; Fist takes no damage; boards burn and go out; Laura stays defiant; Dork is cuffed.
• Check against earlier books: that blue shrinks and red restores, that Laura's attack is lava from her mouth, and that Flaming Head's absence has been explained by this point. I inferred all three from Chapters 11 and 13.
• Open choice: the pigeon gag is a throwaway. Cut it if the tone is too jokey for a final fight.
