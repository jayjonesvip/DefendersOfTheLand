# Book Three — developing ideas

The author is collecting ideas until the story takes shape. These notes preserve supplied details without treating every possibility as a settled chapter or completed manuscript event.

## Lava Lips

- The person Flaming Head has been helping is Laura Baxter, known as Lava Lips. Her real name is author-confirmed. Her age is unspecified; the visual concept depicts an adult woman.
- On the night of the original blast, she was crying beside the lake because her boyfriend had dumped her. Waves crashed and lake water entered her mouth, exposing her to the energy.
- She discovers the effect after brushing her teeth: when she spits, she burns a hole in the sink.
- She seeks help. Everything appears normal while her saliva stays inside her mouth; once it leaves, it burns. She can spit burning plasma.
- She cannot kiss someone without hurting them. This deepens her isolation and despair after the breakup.
- Flaming Head tries to teach her to use her powers to help, but she is too overwhelmed by despair. The exact incidents behind his worried return remain to be developed.
- Stories about a woman who spits burning plasma reach Dork. He believes she could be useful to his team. A newspaper names her Lava Lips after the accidental newsstand fire.
- Current author-approved naming supersedes the earlier Luke-origin idea: a sneeze ignites a newsstand and the newspaper headline dubs her “Lava Lips.” Luke may later use the already-public name sarcastically during a jealous outburst. Laura helps the seller and bystanders get clear; the fire is accidental, and the name humiliates her.
- Earlier discussion suggested a turn toward evil. Preserve that as a possible direction, without treating despair itself as a villainous act or inventing specific crimes.

## Possible motive — blaming Flaming Head

Laura may come to see Flaming Head as the source of a curse rather than a rescuer. Training helps her use the plasma but does not give back the ordinary life and intimacy she wants. She becomes angry at him and wants revenge. Dork finds her at that emotionally vulnerable moment and offers an alliance that appeals to her grievance. His exact approach remains open; no deliberate cursing by Flaming Head or actual cure is established.

- Costume following Flaming Head's involvement: fitted black bodysuit with flame designs up the sides, brown hair in a ponytail, black mask around the eyes.
- Match the established warm, detailed inked comic illustration style. Costume is a design concept, not proof of a completed recruitment or allegiance.

## Story questions left open

- Her civilian identity and life beyond the breakup; who she sought help from and what they could establish.
- Control, range and practical limits of the plasma; how she manages daily life.
- Specific events reported as Lava Lips stories, and how Dork hears them.
- Whether Flaming Head returns alone to report his fears or brings her with him.
- Her choices concerning the Defenders and Dork, and the eventual place of this thread in the book.

## Latest direction under consideration — Laura instead of Bob

The author is considering a smaller villain cast, with Laura taking the major new-character role and Bob potentially omitted. This is a developing direction, not a completed rewrite of the ten-chapter outline.

- Flaming Head's alarm could come from learning that Laura is joining forces with Dork. This gives his return a concrete personal cause: someone he tried to help is aligning with an established enemy.
- Laura's isolation, powers and relationship with Flaming Head provide an existing emotional story. Develop what she wants from Dork and why she accepts his offer, rather than treating despair as sufficient explanation for allegiance.
- Jaws O' Death may like Laura. Whether this is affection, attraction or admiration, and how Laura responds, remain open. Her dangerous saliva gives potential intimacy an obstacle, not an automatic cure through Jaws's iron teeth.
- If Bob is removed, keep the Army ray, tank theft and relocated lab if useful. Remove the sleeping passenger, permanent miniature mercenary and his backstory chapter rather than leaving them as established events.
- Luke's resentment of Dork's growing connection with Laura could replace his jealousy of Bob as the cause of biting the ray. Alternatively, Jaws could develop affection after the electrical accident; choose the sequence before rewriting chapters.
- Do not assume the electrical accident has happened at the time of Flaming Head's return until the revised chronology is settled.

The author accepted this direction.
